# S24 fine-myeloid-subtype sensitivity protocol

Locked: 2026-09-10, before outcome estimates were inspected.

## Objective

Assess whether the within-sample association between SIGLEC15 RNA detectability and the ACP5/MMP9 core is explained solely by differences in fine myeloid-subtype composition.

## Data and annotation

- Processed tumour single-cell objects: GSE155698, GSE156405 and GSE212966.
- Fine subtype labels are restored by exact cell-barcode matching to `Task5_myeloid_subtypes/myeloid_combined_obs.csv`.
- The nine-label annotation was generated by assigning each myeloid cell to its highest marker-module score. The marker definitions contain neither SIGLEC15 nor ACP5/MMP9. It is an algorithmic exploratory annotation, not an externally validated cell atlas label.
- All barcode matches, duplicates and unmatched cells will be reported.

## Frozen exposure and outcome

- Exposure: stored normalised SIGLEC15 expression greater than zero, unchanged from the main analysis.
- Outcome: ACP5/MMP9 core score. ACP5 and MMP9 are z-standardised across all eligible myeloid cells within each biological sample and averaged. This retains the scale of the original cellular analysis before conditioning on subtype.
- A sample first satisfies the parent analysis rule of at least 50 tumour-myeloid cells. Sample-level contrasts additionally require at least one SIGLEC15-detectable and one non-detectable cell. No sample is selected using an observed effect.

## Estimands

1. Fine-subtype-adjusted sample coefficient: within each eligible sample, ordinary least squares of ACP5/MMP9 score on SIGLEC15 detectability and categorical fine subtype. The coefficient on SIGLEC15 detectability is retained. Categories absent from a sample are irrelevant; the most frequent observed category is the reference.
2. Fine-subtype-and-depth-adjusted sample coefficient: the same model additionally includes log1p(total counts), log1p(detected genes) and mitochondrial percentage after within-sample standardisation. Rank-deficient nuisance terms are handled by the least-squares solution; the exposure coefficient is retained only when both exposure groups are present.
3. TREM2+ TAM-restricted contrast: within samples containing both exposure groups among cells labelled TREM2_TAM, compute the detectable-minus-non-detectable mean difference using the full-myeloid sample-standardised ACP5/MMP9 score. A depth-adjusted TREM2+ TAM coefficient is also estimated with the three technical covariates.

## Inference and reporting

- The biological sample remains the inferential unit.
- Sample estimates are equally weighted.
- Ninety-five percent confidence intervals use 10,000 dataset-stratified patient bootstrap resamples with seed 10092026.
- Two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank p values are reported across sample estimates.
- Dataset-specific means and counts are descriptive.
- Results are explicitly post hoc sensitivity analyses. They may reduce or sustain concern about subtype composition but cannot validate fine labels, protein expression, treatment response or patient-selection performance.

## Stop and interpretation rules

- If cell barcodes do not match uniquely, no analysis will be reported.
- If fewer than two datasets or fewer than ten biological samples are estimable for an estimand, only sample-level results and counts will be reported; no pooled inferential conclusion will be made.
- A positive point estimate alone is not called robust. Direction, confidence interval, signed-rank result, sample counts and sensitivity to adjustment are reported together.

## Implementation amendment after the first dry run

The first execution exposed an estimability issue before results were accepted: a least-squares solver can return a minimum-norm exposure coefficient even when SIGLEC15 detectability is completely predicted by the subtype indicators. Such a number is not a within-subtype estimand. The implementation was therefore corrected to require residual exposure variation after projection on the nuisance design (equivalently, at least one subtype containing both detectable and non-detectable cells). Non-identifiable sample coefficients are recorded as missing. This amendment narrows eligibility for the subtype-adjusted estimand and does not select samples by effect direction or magnitude.

## Explicitly post-result diagnostics

After the S24 estimates were obtained, two influence checks were added because the parent analysis had already identified one extreme sample. These checks are descriptive and were not locked before S24 outcome inspection: (i) re-estimation after excluding the sample with the largest absolute unadjusted ACP5/MMP9 contrast; and (ii) leave-one-dataset-out summaries for the fine-subtype-adjusted and fine-subtype-plus-depth-adjusted coefficients. They assess concentration of the result but do not change S24 eligibility or its post hoc evidentiary role.
